What is Artificial Intelligence? 

• Definition: AI lets machines mimic human intelligence using data and patterns instead of strict step-by-step programming. 
• Core Idea: Instead of telling a computer every single rule, developers feed it millions of examples so it can learn on its own. 
• Simulated Traits: Modern systems can see images, understand speech, translate languages, and make autonomous decisions. 

How AI Works 

• Data Collection: Systems require massive amounts of data to learn. 
• Machine Learning (ML): A subset of AI where computers spot patterns in data and improve over time without extra programming. 
• Deep Learning: Advanced neural networks modeled loosely on the human brain that process complex inputs like audio or deep visual data. 

Main Types and Uses of AI 

• Narrow AI: Systems designed to handle one specific task, like spam filters, chess engines, or voice assistants. 
• Generative AI: Models like Google Gemini or other large language models that create new text, images, or code based on prompts. 
• Everyday Examples: GPS route mapping, online shopping suggestions, and facial recognition on phones. 

Benefits and Challenges 

• Advantages: Speeds up medical research, automates repetitive tasks, and solves complex logistical problems. 
• Risks: Job displacement concerns, data privacy issues, and the potential misuse of automated content. 
• Responsible Use: Ensuring safety, fairness, and transparency so AI benefits society as a whole.
